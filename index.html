02Why this program gets you there

This is not a generic routine — it's periodized the way a physique coach preps an actor for a shirtless role. Every aesthetic muscle (upper chest, side delts, lats, arms, abs) is trained twice a week in the 10–20 hard-sets sweet spot that drives growth, with chest and shoulders carrying the most volume because that's what reads on camera and fills a shirt — your edge over the leaner Durden look.

The 6-day split is Push (chest) · Pull (width) · Legs · Push (shoulders) · Pull (thickness + arms) · Arms / Abs / Conditioning · Rest. Movement variations get harder each phase (push-ups → weighted dips; pull-ups → weighted), and the loading scheme rotates weekly — strength → hypertrophy → volume → pump — so you never plateau. Progressive overload + enough protein + the cut/build/cut calorie timing is, mechanically, how the body in your head gets built. Follow it and it does not miss.

03Six truths

01Abs = muscle + low fat

Build the bricks with weighted core work; the deficit sandblasts off what covers them. You can't spot-reduce love handles — only an overall deficit does.

02Chest & shoulders sell it

Upper-chest shelf + capped side delts over a tight waist is the model illusion. That's why they get the most volume — and why we keep obliques light (heavy side work thickens the waist).

03Protein is non-negotiable

~160–165g daily protects muscle while you lose fat. The food pages do the maths for you around your whey.

04Progressive overload

Beat last week — a rep, a kilo, a harder variation. Logged. This single habit is what actually builds the physique.

05Sleep & recovery

7–9 hrs. Poor sleep spikes cortisol (belly fat) and tanks your lifts. Deloads (wk 8, 18) are where you grow.

06Trend > daily noise

Lower abs surface last, in the final phase. The graph slopes down over months, not days. Don't panic mid-way.

One health note

You're 24, healthy weight, chasing a defined ~9–10% — lean but sound, not extreme. Don't chase sub-8% (miserable, photographs worse), don't crash-diet, and if you're persistently exhausted or your lifts crater for weeks, eat more, not less. A doctor / sports-dietitian check before you start and bloodwork at the midpoint (B12, iron, vit D — important since you're going meat-light) is smart insurance.

◇The Sunday cook — oven-only, ~40 min hands-off

1 · Overnight oats ×6 jars no cooking · ~40g protein
Per jar: 50g oats · 200ml milk · 1 scoop whey · 100g Greek yogurt · handful frozen berries
  1. Spoon everything into 6 tubs, stir, lid on, fridge.
  2. Grab one each morning, eat cold. Zero cooking.
  3. Prefer eggs? 3–4 boiled or oven-baked eggs + a banana is a fine breakfast too (step 3).
2 · One oven tray — chicken + potato + veg ~35 min · the only real cooking
1.5kg Carrefour Tender Chicken Breast (frozen, boneless, skinless, ~AED 22/kg) · 2kg potatoes · 3 onions · 2 garlic bulbs · frozen veg · olive oil · salt · pepper · your spice blend
  1. Chicken: thaw overnight in the fridge, then pat dry with paper towel — dry chicken browns, wet chicken just steams. Slice any very thick breast in half horizontally so it cooks evenly.
  2. Potatoes: scrub, leave the skin on, cut into even ~2–3 cm wedges so they all roast at the same speed.
  3. Onions: peel, halve from top to root, then cut each half into 3–4 thick wedges — chunky, not thin (thin slices burn before the chicken is done). They turn soft and sweet.
  4. Garlic — two easy ways: for sweet, mellow cloves, smash each one flat with the side of a knife, leave the skin on, and scatter them through the tray. For punchier flavour, peel and finely grate or crush 4–6 cloves straight into the oil + spice mix so it coats everything.
  5. Assemble: toss chicken, wedges, onions & veg in 1–2 tbsp oil + your blend, then spread in a single layer — do not crowd the tray (use two if needed, or it steams instead of roasting).
  6. Roast at 200°C for ~30–35 min, until the chicken is cooked through and the wedges are golden at the edges.
  7. Cool, then split into ~6 boxes. Meat-light swap: roast 2 tins of drained chickpeas with the same spices for your no-meat days.
3 · A dozen eggs — boiled or oven-baked breakfast + snacks
12 eggs
  1. Boil 10 min, OR bake in a muffin tray at 160°C for ~12–15 min — no pot, fully oven.
  2. Cool, peel, fridge. 2 eggs ≈ 12g protein; 3–4 make a breakfast.
  3. Don’t eat them raw — salmonella risk, and you absorb only ~half the protein raw vs ~90% cooked. Always cook them.

◇Tuna days — make it great, or swap it out

Make tinned tuna taste great 2 min · no cooking
Drain it well first — watery tuna is bland tuna. Then build it up:
  1. Bind with Greek yogurt instead of mayo — creamy, lean, and extra protein.
  2. Acid + aromatics: a squeeze of lemon, finely chopped onion, one crushed garlic clove, chilli flakes, black pepper.
  3. Spoon in a spice blend from your library — harissa, za’atar, cajun, or a dash of sriracha all transform it.
  4. Add volume & texture: stir through chickpeas, sweetcorn, or diced cucumber & tomato.
  5. Try a combo — Mediterranean: tuna + chickpeas + lemon + olive oil + red onion + parsley · Spicy: tuna + harissa + lime + spring onion · Curried: tuna + ½ tsp curry powder + yogurt + lime.
  6. Eat it warm: pile it onto oven wedges and grill 2 min, or fold it through the roasted veg from your tray.

Bored of tuna? Rotate these in — all cheap, lean and on the shelf at Carrefour. One is suggested on each week’s food page so you are never eating the same thing twice in a row.

◇Season it — flavour library

Two blends per cook keeps the week interesting — one per tray half, on both the chicken and the veg. Each one rotates onto a week automatically (see any week's food page). Everything here is cheap and on the shelf at Carrefour or any Indian / Middle-Eastern grocer.

5 max-flavour rules: 1) Season boldly — under-salting is the No.1 cause of bland food. 2) Toss everything in 1–2 tbsp oil so the spices stick and the edges crisp. 3) Finish with acid — a squeeze of lemon after roasting makes it pop. 4) Marinate when you can; yogurt-based rubs (tandoori) tenderise and keep chicken juicy. 5) Roast hot at 200°C, do not crowd the tray, and scatter fresh herbs or chilli at the very end.

◇Scale it for the other phases

Build phase (Month 4 · ~2,600 kcal)
Add a 2nd potato portion at lunch, an extra 50g oats in the jar, and a 4th whey scoop. That clears the higher carb & protein numbers.
Peak cut (Months 5–6 · ~2,000 kcal)
Halve the lunch potato, drop the evening snack, and double the veg to stay full on fewer calories. Protein stays high.

◇Your bourbon biscuits — the verdict

Keep the habit, kill the packet

A whole packet every two days is roughly 250–350 kcal a day of sugar and refined flour — on a fat-loss cut that is enough to stall your abs and crowd out the protein you need. You don't have to ban it (being miserable is how diets fail), but swap the packet for a small, counted treat: 2–3 biscuits with your evening coffee, inside your calories. Better swaps that kill the same craving for fewer calories and some protein: 2–3 squares of 85% dark chocolate, Greek yogurt + cocoa + a little honey (chocolate-pudding hit, ~15g protein), a protein bar (pick one under ~15g sugar), or oat cakes + dark chocolate. In the final peak weeks (5–6), tighten to occasional only.

◇Sweet tooth — treats that fit

All built from things already on your list, all high-protein or low-sugar — they kill the craving without denting the deficit.

Chocolate protein mousse ~25g protein · 2 min
200g Greek yogurt + 1 scoop chocolate whey + a splash of milk
  1. Stir hard until thick and smooth — it whips up into a proper mousse.
  2. Top with frozen berries or a few dark-chocolate shavings. This is your default dessert.
Banana “nice cream” 5 min · freezer
1 frozen banana + a splash of milk + 1 tsp cocoa (± ½ scoop whey)
  1. Slice a banana and freeze it in a tub. Blend frozen with a little milk and cocoa until it turns to soft-serve.
Frozen berry-yogurt bark make a batch
Greek yogurt + a little honey + frozen berries
  1. Spread yogurt thin on a lined tray, scatter berries over, freeze, then snap into shards. Grab one when you want something cold and sweet.
Grab-and-go no recipe
When you just want something instant: 2–3 squares of 85% dark chocolate · a protein bar (under ~15g sugar) · 2 dates with a little peanut butter · or your 2–3 bourbon biscuits with coffee, counted.
Rule of thumb: one small sweet a day, kept inside your calories, is completely fine — it is what makes the plan stickable. In the final peak weeks (5–6), lean on the protein mousse and dark chocolate and keep the sugary ones occasional.

◇Avoid / give up

Give up
Sugary drinks — soda, juice, sweetened coffees, energy drinks. Liquid sugar with zero fullness; the single biggest easy win.
Give up
Alcohol (or minimise hard) — empty calories that wreck sleep, recovery and fat loss. Ideally none during the cut.
Give up
Deep-fried & fast food — hidden oils blow the calorie budget in one meal.
Give up
Whole packets of biscuits / pastries / sweets — see above. A couple, counted, is fine; the sleeve is not.
Limit
Calorie-bomb add-ons — mayo, creamy dressings, big spoons of peanut butter, lots of cheese. Easy to overeat without noticing.
Limit
White-bread-heavy meals — swap for potato, oats or a wrap + protein, which keep you fuller.
Lean on
Lean protein, eggs, dairy, whey, veg, fruit, water (3L+), black coffee/tea. High-volume, filling, on-target.

Don't chase perfection — the 80/20 rule (90/10 in the peak weeks) is what makes this last six months. Keep treats small and occasional and you'll still see the abs.
