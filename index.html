02Why this program gets you there

This is not a generic routine — it's periodized the way a physique coach preps an actor for a shirtless role. Every aesthetic muscle (upper chest, side delts, lats, arms, abs) is trained twice a week in the 10–20 hard-sets sweet spot that drives growth, with chest and shoulders carrying the most volume because that's what reads on camera and fills a shirt — and conditioning, calisthenics control and daily mobility keep it the dense-but-athletic Amotti silhouette rather than a stiff gym build.

The 6-day split is Push (chest) · Pull (width) · Legs · Push (shoulders) · Pull (thickness + arms) · Arms / Abs / Conditioning · Rest. Movement variations get harder each phase (push-ups → weighted dips; pull-ups → weighted), and the loading scheme rotates weekly — strength → hypertrophy → volume → pump — so you never plateau. Progressive overload + enough protein + the cut/build/cut calorie timing is, mechanically, how the body in your head gets built. Follow it and it does not miss.

04Your daily base layer

The lifting builds the shape — but these four habits, done every single day, are what actually drive the fat-loss and recovery. They are the part most people skip, so bake them in from day one.

01Steps — 8–10k a day

The biggest fat-loss lever after diet. A desk job in Dubai means your default movement is low, which quietly shrinks your deficit — steps protect it and prevent plateaus. Walk calls, take stairs, park far.

02Treadmill — built into every day

Your office steps are low, so the treadmill does the work. Your protocol is 12-3-30: 12% incline · 4.8 km/h (3 mph) · 30 min after lifting, plus a 20–30 min top-up later (rest days: one 45–60 min 12-3 walk). Slow enough to read or play chess, steep enough to burn — and still Zone 2, so it never taxes recovery. Two rules: don’t hold the rails (that erases 20–30% of the work — fine to hold your phone or a book), and ease in at 10% the first week if 12% bites the calves. Skip long daily hard running.

03Water — 3.5–4 L a day

Higher than the usual 3L because of the heat, six training days, high protein and creatine. Add 300–500ml before a morning session and sip through it. Pale-straw urine means you are hydrated.

04Sleep — 7–8 hrs

The real limiter, especially training before work. Too little spikes cortisol and tanks your lifts. Get to bed earlier rather than cutting recovery short.

One health note

You're 24, healthy weight, chasing a defined ~9–10% — lean but sound, not extreme. Don't chase sub-8% (miserable, photographs worse), don't crash-diet, and if you're persistently exhausted or your lifts crater for weeks, eat more, not less. A doctor / sports-dietitian check before you start and bloodwork at the midpoint (B12, iron, vit D — important since you're going meat-light) is smart insurance.

How hard to push, how fast to lose

Effort: take working sets to about 1–3 reps from failure (RPE 8–9). You don't need to grind every set to failure — the research shows stopping a rep or two short builds nearly the same muscle for far less fatigue. Save true failure for the last set of isolation moves (curls, raises, pushdowns), not the heavy compounds.

Progress: beat last week — one more rep or a little more load. Add a set to a lagging area across a block; the scheduled deloads (wk 8, 13, 18, 26) then clear the fatigue so you actually grow.

Loss rate: aim to lose ~0.5–1% of bodyweight a week (~0.35–0.65 kg). Faster on the scale, or lifts dropping two weeks running, means eat a little more — crash loss burns muscle, not just fat.

Dialled to current sports-science consensus: 10–20 hard sets per muscle per week trained twice weekly, working 1–3 reps from failure, ~1.6–2.2 g/kg protein (higher in a deficit to spare muscle), and a deload every 4–8 weeks. The bulk-then-cut arc mirrors how physique coaches prep actors for shirtless roles.

◇The Sunday cook — oven-only, ~40 min hands-off

1 · Overnight oats ×6 jars no cooking · ~40g protein
Per jar: 50g oats · 200ml milk · 1 scoop whey · 100g Greek yogurt · handful frozen berries
  1. Spoon everything into 6 tubs, stir, lid on, fridge.
  2. Grab one each morning, eat cold. Zero cooking.
  3. Prefer eggs? 3–4 boiled or oven-baked eggs + a banana is a fine breakfast too (step 3).
2 · One oven tray — chicken + potato + veg ~35 min · the only real cooking
1.5kg Carrefour Tender Chicken Breast (frozen, boneless, skinless, ~AED 22/kg) · 2kg potatoes · 3 onions · 2 garlic bulbs · frozen veg · olive oil · salt · pepper · your spice blend
  1. Chicken: thaw overnight in the fridge, then pat dry with paper towel — dry chicken browns, wet chicken just steams. Slice any very thick breast in half horizontally so it cooks evenly.
  2. Potatoes: scrub, leave the skin on, cut into even ~2–3 cm wedges so they all roast at the same speed.
  3. Onions: peel, halve from top to root, then cut each half into 3–4 thick wedges — chunky, not thin (thin slices burn before the chicken is done). They turn soft and sweet.
  4. Garlic — two easy ways: for sweet, mellow cloves, smash each one flat with the side of a knife, leave the skin on, and scatter them through the tray. For punchier flavour, peel and finely grate or crush 4–6 cloves straight into the oil + spice mix so it coats everything.
  5. Assemble: toss chicken, wedges, onions & veg in 1–2 tbsp oil + your blend, then spread in a single layer — do not crowd the tray (use two if needed, or it steams instead of roasting).
  6. Roast at 200°C for ~30–35 min, until the chicken is cooked through and the wedges are golden at the edges.
  7. Cool, then split into ~6 boxes. Meat-light swap: roast 2 tins of drained chickpeas with the same spices for your no-meat days.
3 · A dozen eggs — boiled or oven-baked breakfast + snacks
12 eggs
  1. Boil 10 min, OR bake in a muffin tray at 160°C for ~12–15 min — no pot, fully oven.
  2. Cool, peel, fridge. 2 eggs ≈ 12g protein; 3–4 make a breakfast.
  3. Don’t eat them raw — salmonella risk, and you absorb only ~half the protein raw vs ~90% cooked. Always cook them.

◇Seafood weeks — fish & shrimp (the chicken swap)

On weeks 3, 6, 9, 12, 15, 18, 21 & 24 the main swaps off chicken to fish or shrimp. One real difference from chicken: seafood doesn't keep or reheat as well — cooked fish/shrimp is best within 2–3 days and turns rubbery if you blast it in the microwave. The upside is it cooks in 6–15 min from scratch, so on these weeks either cook a 2–3 day batch plus one quick midweek top-up, or just cook each portion fresh (genuinely ~10 min). Your potato wedges, veg and oats jars stay exactly the same as a normal week.

Salmon — tray-baked ~12–15 min · weeks 9 & 18
~1.2 kg salmon fillets (6 × ~200g, skin-on) · 1 tbsp olive oil · salt · pepper · ½ lemon per fillet · your spice blend
  1. Thaw & dry: if frozen, thaw overnight in the fridge, then pat the fillets really dry — a dry surface browns, a wet one steams.
  2. No cutting needed: keep fillets whole, one ~180–200g piece per box. If a fillet is much thicker at one end, leave it — the thin tail just cooks a touch faster.
  3. Skin on, skin-side down: bake on the skin (it holds the fillet together and peels off easily after). Line the tray with foil or baking paper for zero cleanup.
  4. Season: rub each fillet with a little oil + your blend — lemon & herb, cajun or harissa all suit salmon.
  5. Bake at 200°C for 12–15 min, until it flakes with light fork pressure and is just opaque in the middle. Salmon is best a hair under rather than dried out (~52°C internal for moist, 60°C for fully done).
  6. Finish with a squeeze of lemon. Eat within 2–3 days — cook fresh or in small batches.
White fish — basa / tilapia / cod ~12–18 min · weeks 3, 12 & 21
~1.3 kg white fish fillets (6 × ~200g) · 1 tbsp olive oil · 3–4 cloves garlic · lemon · salt · pepper · your spice blend
  1. Thaw & dry hard: thaw overnight in the fridge, then press with paper towel twice — frozen white fish holds a lot of water, and wet fish steams grey instead of roasting.
  2. One fillet per box (~180–200g): no real cutting. With thin fillets (basa/tilapia), fold the thin tail end under so the piece is an even thickness and cooks uniformly.
  3. Season: brush with oil, crushed garlic and your blend. Lay in a single layer, pieces not touching.
  4. Bake at 200°C: thin fillets (basa/tilapia) ~12 min; thick cod ~15–18 min. Done the moment it turns opaque white and flakes easily.
  5. Handle gently: don't flip it — lift with a fish slice. Finish with lemon.
  6. Very lean, so it overcooks and dries fast — best fresh or within 2 days, and reheat gently and briefly.
Garlic-chilli shrimp / prawns ~6–8 min · weeks 6, 15 & 24
~1 kg raw peeled shrimp (or ~1.3 kg shell-on) · 4–6 cloves garlic · 1 tsp chilli flakes · 1 tbsp olive oil · lemon · salt · parsley
  1. Thaw: overnight in the fridge, or quick-thaw in a bowl of cold water ~15 min. Drain and pat dry.
  2. Buy peeled & deveined to skip the work — ~1 kg peeled ≈ 6 servings. If shell-on: pull off the legs and shell, then run a knife shallowly along the back and lift out the dark vein.
  3. No cutting — leave them whole. Toss with oil, crushed garlic, chilli and salt.
  4. Bake at 200°C for just 6–8 min, until they turn pink and curl into a loose C. Do not overcook — a tight O-curl means rubbery. (On a pan: 2–3 min a side, even faster.)
  5. Finish with lemon and parsley.
  6. Shrimp are the fastest of everything here — genuinely cook these fresh each time; it's quicker than reheating.
Seafood storage & safety: cool within an hour of cooking and keep in airtight boxes — cooked fish 2–3 days, shrimp 2 days. Always thaw frozen seafood in the fridge, never on the counter. If you do batch-cook, pull it from the oven slightly underdone so the reheat doesn't turn it rubbery. Buy "individually frozen" (IQF) fillets/shrimp so you can take out exactly what a day needs.

◇Tuna days — make it great, or swap it out

Make tinned tuna taste great 2 min · no cooking
Drain it well first — watery tuna is bland tuna. Then build it up:
  1. Bind with Greek yogurt instead of mayo — creamy, lean, and extra protein.
  2. Acid + aromatics: a squeeze of lemon, finely chopped onion, one crushed garlic clove, chilli flakes, black pepper.
  3. Spoon in a spice blend from your library — harissa, za’atar, cajun, or a dash of sriracha all transform it.
  4. Add volume & texture: stir through chickpeas, sweetcorn, or diced cucumber & tomato.
  5. Try a combo — Mediterranean: tuna + chickpeas + lemon + olive oil + red onion + parsley · Spicy: tuna + harissa + lime + spring onion · Curried: tuna + ½ tsp curry powder + yogurt + lime.
  6. Eat it warm: pile it onto oven wedges and grill 2 min, or fold it through the roasted veg from your tray.

Bored of tuna? Rotate these in — all cheap, lean and on the shelf at Carrefour. One is suggested on each week’s food page so you are never eating the same thing twice in a row.

◇Season it — flavour library

Two blends per cook keeps the week interesting — one per tray half, on both the chicken and the veg. Each one rotates onto a week automatically (see any week's food page). Everything here is cheap and on the shelf at Carrefour or any Indian / Middle-Eastern grocer.

5 max-flavour rules: 1) Season boldly — under-salting is the No.1 cause of bland food. 2) Toss everything in 1–2 tbsp oil so the spices stick and the edges crisp. 3) Finish with acid — a squeeze of lemon after roasting makes it pop. 4) Marinate when you can; yogurt-based rubs (tandoori) tenderise and keep chicken juicy. 5) Roast hot at 200°C, do not crowd the tray, and scatter fresh herbs or chilli at the very end.

◇Scale it for the other phases

Add a 2nd potato portion at lunch, an extra 50g oats in the jar, and a 4th whey scoop. That clears the higher carb & protein numbers.

Halve the lunch potato, drop the evening snack, and double the veg to stay full on fewer calories. Protein stays high.

◇Your bourbon biscuits — the verdict

Keep the habit, kill the packet

A whole packet every two days is roughly 250–350 kcal a day of sugar and refined flour — on a fat-loss cut that is enough to stall your abs and crowd out the protein you need. You don't have to ban it (being miserable is how diets fail), but swap the packet for a small, counted treat: 2–3 biscuits with your evening coffee, inside your calories. Better swaps that kill the same craving for fewer calories and some protein: 2–3 squares of 85% dark chocolate, Greek yogurt + cocoa + a little honey (chocolate-pudding hit, ~15g protein), a protein bar (pick one under ~15g sugar), or oat cakes + dark chocolate. In the final peak weeks (5–6), tighten to occasional only.

◇Avoid / give up

Give up
Sugary drinks — soda, juice, sweetened coffees, energy drinks. Liquid sugar with zero fullness; the single biggest easy win.
Give up
Alcohol (or minimise hard) — empty calories that wreck sleep, recovery and fat loss. Ideally none during the cut.
Give up
Deep-fried & fast food — hidden oils blow the calorie budget in one meal.
Give up
Whole packets of biscuits / pastries / sweets — see above. A couple, counted, is fine; the sleeve is not.
Limit
Calorie-bomb add-ons — mayo, creamy dressings, big spoons of peanut butter, lots of cheese. Easy to overeat without noticing.
Limit
White-bread-heavy meals — swap for potato, oats or a wrap + protein, which keep you fuller.
Lean on
Lean protein, eggs, dairy, whey, veg, fruit, water (3L+), black coffee/tea. High-volume, filling, on-target.

Don't chase perfection — the 80/20 rule (90/10 in the peak weeks) is what makes this last six months. Keep treats small and occasional and you'll still see the abs.
