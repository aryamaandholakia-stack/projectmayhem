The Target

Brad Pitt in Fight Club was roughly your bodyweight — around 68–70 kg — but at an estimated 5–8% body fat. That's stage-lean and hard to live at. Your camera-ready target is ~9–10%: that's the band where a full six-pack reads — including the stubborn lower two. Abs first start peeking around 12%, but the bottom of the stomach only sharpens in the single digits, so that's where we're aiming. Defined, not bulky — the look that pays.

The Training Week

The honest answer to "do the exercises change?": the 7-day split stays identical all 26 weeks — same muscle group on the same day. What changes by phase is the difficulty of each movement and the intensity. You ride the easy variation up to the brutal one (push-ups → archer → weighted) and add load as you get stronger. That fixed skeleton is exactly what makes progress measurable. Pick any week below — it jumps you to that week's variations, intensity, diet target and an end-of-week weigh-in.

How to progress — the one rule that matters

Progressive overload. Each week, beat last week by a little: one more rep, a harder variation, slightly more weight, or a touch less rest. When a calisthenics exercise gets easy (hitting the top of the rep range across all sets), advance to the harder variation. When a weighted lift's top range feels like RPE 7, add load. Log every session below — what gets measured gets built. This single habit is what separates a transformation from a year of treading water.

The abs protocol — your non-negotiable

A six-pack is two things stacked: thick ab muscle + low enough body fat to see it. Your plan already trains core 4×/week (Thu, Fri, Sat, Tue) — the rule is to train abs like any other muscle: with added resistance and progression, not endless light crunches. Anchor every core block with one weighted, low-rep movement (cable/plate crunch or weighted hanging leg raise, 3×8–12, RPE 9) to build size, then finish with bodyweight volume (hollow holds, planks, leg raises). Hit the lower abs hard (hanging leg/knee raises, reverse crunches) — that's the region that shows last and matters most for the full six. But understand the order of operations: no amount of ab work reveals a six-pack on its own — the deficit does that. Build the bricks now; the diet sandblasts off what's covering them. By ~9–10% they're all visible.

Shoot wk
Final week — keep it simple

No crash diets or dehydration tricks. Hold your routine, keep water normal, get sleep. A lean, well-fed, well-rested look photographs far better than a depleted one.

Eat these — your building blocks

  • Lean protein chicken, white fish, lean beef, eggs, Greek yogurt, tofu, whey
  • Smart carbs oats, rice, quinoa, sweet potato, fruit, legumes
  • Good fats olive oil, avocado, nuts, oily fish
  • Volume veg leafy greens, peppers, broccoli — eat freely
  • Water 3L+ daily · fat loss & fullness

Go easy on these

  • Alcohol stalls fat loss + spikes appetite
  • Liquid calories juices, lattes, soft drinks
  • Refined carbs & sugar keep occasional, not daily
  • Fried / takeaway hidden calories wreck a deficit
  • Late heavy meals hurt sleep = hurt your midsection

Supplements — only the proven few

05

Eight Truths

The things that decide whether this works. Tape these to your mirror.

01You can't spot-reduce

No amount of side crunches melts love handles. Direct ab work builds the muscle; the overall calorie deficit uncovers it. Your belly is the last place to lean out — that's biology, not failure. Stay the course.

02Protein is king

In a deficit, high protein (~150g+) is what tells your body to burn fat and keep muscle. Miss this and you'll get smaller but still soft. Hit it every single day.

03Sleep is a training variable

7–9 hours. Under-sleeping raises cortisol, which parks fat on your midsection and saps your lifts. Your rest day's job is partly to bank great sleep.

04The skinny-fat advantage

As a beginner you get "newbie gains" — building muscle and losing fat at once, which trained lifters can't. This window is gold; train hard and eat enough protein to spend it well.

05Track, don't guess

Weekly: front/side/back photos in the same light, waist tape at the navel, and your gym logbook. The mirror and the tape tell the truth the scale hides.

06Consistency > intensity

Six honest 70-min sessions a week for 26 weeks beats two heroic ones. Don't go to failure on everything early — leave reps in the tank, recover, repeat, advance.

07Width sells the look

The Durden illusion is shoulders + back over a tight waist. Prioritise lateral raises, pull-ups, and rows — they create the taper that makes the waist look smaller.

08These are estimates

Body-fat timelines vary by genetics, sleep, stress, and adherence. If month 1 lands at 22% not 21%, adjust calories down ~150 and keep moving. Trends matter, not single numbers.

One health note

You're 24, at a healthy weight, chasing a defined ~9–10% with a visible six-pack — lean, but a sound and achievable goal, not an extreme one. Keep it that way: ~9–10% is the destination, so don't chase shredded single digits below ~8% — that's competition territory, miserable to hold, and it photographs worse, not better. Don't crash-diet, and if you ever feel persistently exhausted, foggy, moody, or your lifts crater for weeks, that's a signal to eat a bit more, not less. Given you're optimising hard for six months, a quick check-in with a doctor or sports dietitian before you start — and bloodwork around the halfway mark — is a smart, cheap insurance policy.
